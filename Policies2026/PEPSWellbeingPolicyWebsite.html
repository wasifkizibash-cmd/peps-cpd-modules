Section 1

Purpose of the Policy

At Providence English Private School (PEPS), the wellbeing of every member of our school community is an essential part of our educational purpose.

PEPS is committed to creating a safe, respectful, inclusive and supportive environment in which students and adults can learn, work, develop positive relationships and achieve their potential.

Our approach recognises that wellbeing is not limited to the absence of mental health difficulties. It includes the emotional, social, physical, mental, academic and relational aspects of an individual's life.

The school's 2023–2024 policy established a whole-school approach to positive mental health and wellbeing and recognised that wellbeing is the responsibility of the entire school community. This revised policy builds on that foundation and extends it into a broader whole-school wellbeing framework for 2026–2027.

This policy is designed to align with the SPEA Student Wellbeing Census, CIS standards of outstanding wellbeing and the Wellbeing performance standard within the UAE School Inspection Framework, and will be reviewed against any updated regulatory guidance issued by SPEA.

Section 2

PEPS Definition of Wellbeing

At PEPS, wellbeing is understood as the extent to which individuals feel safe, valued, connected, healthy, supported and able to participate, learn, contribute and flourish.

Wellbeing is influenced by both the individual and the environment in which they live, learn and work. PEPS recognises six interconnected dimensions of wellbeing:

2.1

Emotional

Developing emotional awareness, self-regulation, resilience, confidence and healthy ways of managing challenges.

2.2

Social

Developing positive relationships, friendships, communication skills, respect, empathy and a sense of belonging.

2.3

Physical

Promoting healthy lifestyles, physical activity, appropriate nutrition, hydration, rest, personal health and physical safety.

2.4

Mental

Promoting positive mental health, coping strategies, help-seeking behaviour and comfort discussing concerns.

2.5

Academic

Supporting confidence as learners, managing academic demands and responding positively to setbacks.

2.6

Purpose & Agency

Developing a sense of purpose, responsibility, self-worth, aspiration and positive personal choices.

Section 3

Policy Statement

At PEPS, we are committed to promoting the positive wellbeing of our whole school community, including:

  • Students
  • Teaching staff
  • Non-teaching staff
  • School leaders
  • Parents and carers

We recognise that everyone experiences challenges at different stages of life and that individuals may require additional support at different times. We therefore aim to:

  • create a caring, respectful and inclusive school culture;
  • promote positive relationships and a sense of belonging;
  • help students develop resilience and emotional literacy;
  • provide opportunities for students to develop agency and responsibility;
  • promote healthy physical and digital lifestyles;
  • support academic confidence and healthy approaches to learning;
  • identify emerging wellbeing concerns at an early stage;
  • provide appropriate support and intervention;
  • work collaboratively with parents and carers;
  • support staff wellbeing;
  • work with appropriate external professionals where required; and
  • regularly review and improve our wellbeing provision.

Our approach is based on the principle that wellbeing is everyone's responsibility.

Section 5

Promoting a Positive Wellbeing Culture

PEPS will promote a mentally and physically healthy environment by:

  • promoting the school's values;
  • creating a strong sense of belonging;
  • developing respectful relationships;
  • celebrating academic and non-academic achievements;
  • providing opportunities for responsibility and leadership;
  • encouraging student voice;
  • providing opportunities for reflection;
  • promoting positive and restorative approaches to behaviour;
  • providing access to appropriate support;
  • promoting healthy lifestyles;
  • promoting positive digital behaviours;
  • providing opportunities for physical activity;
  • recognising the importance of staff wellbeing;
  • providing appropriate wellbeing education;
  • reducing stigma associated with seeking help; and
  • creating an environment in which individuals feel safe to express concerns.

A supportive ethos, restorative approaches to behaviour, positive relationships, emotional skills, resilience and an open culture around mental health remain central principles of this policy.

Section 6

Wellbeing Through Teaching and Learning

Wellbeing education will be integrated into students' educational experience rather than treated as a separate activity. Appropriate wellbeing learning may be provided through:

  • PSHE;
  • pastoral programmes;
  • assemblies;
  • teaching activities;
  • curriculum lessons;
  • PE and physical activity;
  • careers and guidance;
  • enrichment activities;
  • school campaigns;
  • wellbeing events;
  • student leadership activities; and
  • age-appropriate discussions and activities.

Emotional and mental health learning is embedded within the developmental PSHE curriculum. The specific content and approach will be adapted to the developmental needs of each year group.

Section 7

Student Voice, Agency and Leadership

PEPS recognises that students should not only receive wellbeing support but should also have an active role in shaping their own wellbeing and that of the school community. The school will provide opportunities for students to:

  • express their views;
  • communicate concerns;
  • participate in decision-making;
  • contribute to wellbeing initiatives;
  • take leadership responsibilities;
  • participate in student-led campaigns;
  • support positive peer relationships;
  • suggest improvements to the school environment; and
  • develop confidence in seeking appropriate help.

Student voice may be gathered through existing school structures and activities, including anonymous channels where appropriate, without creating unnecessary additional administrative requirements.

Section 8

Student Physical Wellbeing

PEPS will promote healthy physical development and lifestyle choices through:

  • physical education and sport;
  • opportunities for physical activity;
  • healthy eating education;
  • hydration;
  • health and hygiene education;
  • appropriate rest and recovery;
  • health and safety education;
  • awareness of healthy lifestyle choices; and
  • access to appropriate health support through the school nurse and relevant staff.

Physical wellbeing will be promoted in ways that are appropriate to students' ages and developmental stages.

Section 9

Digital Wellbeing

Recognising the significant role technology plays in students' lives, PEPS will promote responsible and healthy use of digital technology. Students will be supported to understand:

  • responsible technology use;
  • online safety;
  • cyberbullying;
  • respectful online communication;
  • privacy and personal information;
  • digital identity and digital footprints;
  • appropriate use of social media;
  • managing digital distractions;
  • healthy relationships with technology;
  • the relationship between device use, sleep and wellbeing;
  • responsible use of artificial intelligence and digital tools; and
  • how and where to seek help when online experiences negatively affect wellbeing.

Digital wellbeing will be addressed through appropriate curriculum, pastoral, safeguarding and behaviour provision.

Section 10

Academic Wellbeing

PEPS recognises that academic experiences can significantly influence student wellbeing. The school will promote academic wellbeing by helping students:

  • develop confidence as learners;
  • set realistic and meaningful goals;
  • develop effective study habits;
  • manage workload;
  • respond constructively to mistakes and setbacks;
  • develop resilience;
  • prepare appropriately for assessments and examinations;
  • manage examination-related stress;
  • make informed academic choices;
  • prepare for transitions between year groups;
  • make informed IGCSE and post-16 decisions; and
  • understand that seeking academic support is a positive behaviour.

Teachers and pastoral staff will remain alert to significant changes in engagement, motivation, achievement or behaviour that may indicate a wellbeing concern.

Section 11

Staff Wellbeing

PEPS recognises that staff wellbeing is fundamental to the wellbeing of students and the effectiveness of the school. The school will seek to promote staff wellbeing through:

  • respectful professional relationships;
  • supportive leadership;
  • appropriate induction and orientation;
  • opportunities for professional development;
  • recognition of staff contributions;
  • opportunities for collaboration;
  • access to appropriate support;
  • encouraging staff to communicate concerns;
  • promoting reasonable approaches to workload;
  • supporting staff during periods of increased professional pressure; and
  • developing a culture in which seeking support is viewed positively.

All staff are encouraged to take reasonable responsibility for their own wellbeing and to seek support when required. Staff should also remain attentive to the wellbeing of colleagues and communicate concerns through appropriate leadership channels.

Section 12

Inclusion and Wellbeing

PEPS recognises that inclusion and wellbeing are closely connected. The school will seek to ensure that wellbeing provision is accessible and appropriate for students with different:

  • learning needs;
  • abilities;
  • communication needs;
  • cultural backgrounds;
  • language needs;
  • social and emotional needs; and
  • individual circumstances.

The Wellbeing Team will work with the Inclusion/Learning Support Team and relevant staff to ensure that appropriate adjustments and support are considered where necessary. This policy should be read alongside the school's Inclusion/SEND, Safeguarding and Child Protection, Anti-Bullying and Behaviour policies.

Section 13

Transition and Change

Transitions and significant changes can affect wellbeing. PEPS will provide appropriate support during transitions, including:

  • students joining the school;
  • movement between key stages;
  • transition from primary to secondary;
  • transition into examination years;
  • IGCSE subject choices;
  • transition from Year 11 to post-16 pathways;
  • students returning following extended absence;
  • significant changes affecting individual students; and
  • transition and induction of new staff.

Support may include orientation, communication with relevant staff and parents, pastoral check-ins, information sharing and additional monitoring where appropriate.

Section 14

Roles and Responsibilities

14.1 Principal

The Principal has overall responsibility for ensuring that wellbeing is recognised as an important part of the school's culture and strategic direction.

14.2 Senior Leadership Team

The SLT will:

  • support implementation of this policy;
  • ensure appropriate resources and structures;
  • respond to significant wellbeing concerns;
  • support staff wellbeing;
  • review wellbeing priorities; and
  • ensure appropriate links with other school policies.

14.3 Wellbeing Lead / Wellbeing Team

The Wellbeing Lead and Wellbeing Team will:

  • coordinate whole-school wellbeing provision;
  • work with relevant staff to promote positive wellbeing;
  • advise staff on wellbeing concerns;
  • coordinate appropriate wellbeing activities;
  • support the delivery of wellbeing education;
  • coordinate appropriate referrals;
  • liaise with parents where appropriate;
  • support staff training and awareness; and
  • contribute to the annual review of the policy.

14.4 Safeguarding/Child Protection Lead

The Safeguarding Lead will manage concerns where wellbeing issues may involve safeguarding risk. Where a wellbeing concern presents a safeguarding risk, the school's Safeguarding and Child Protection procedures will take precedence.

14.5 Teachers and Other Staff

All staff are expected to:

  • promote positive wellbeing;
  • model respectful and healthy behaviours;
  • develop positive relationships with students;
  • remain alert to changes in student wellbeing;
  • listen appropriately to concerns;
  • communicate concerns through the appropriate channels; and
  • participate in relevant wellbeing activities and training.

All staff share responsibility for monitoring students' wellbeing and referring concerns where appropriate.

14.6 Students

Students are encouraged to:

  • take reasonable responsibility for their own wellbeing;
  • treat others respectfully;
  • participate positively in wellbeing activities;
  • communicate concerns;
  • seek help when needed; and
  • contribute to a positive school environment.

14.7 Parents and Carers

Parents and carers are encouraged to:

  • support positive wellbeing practices at home;
  • communicate concerns to the school;
  • work collaboratively with relevant staff;
  • participate in appropriate workshops and guidance;
  • support healthy routines and responsible digital behaviours; and
  • work with the school where additional support is required.
Section 15

Early Identification

PEPS recognises that early identification can prevent concerns from becoming more serious. All staff should remain alert to changes in:

  • attendance;
  • punctuality;
  • relationships;
  • engagement with learning;
  • academic performance;
  • behaviour;
  • mood;
  • social interaction;
  • physical presentation;
  • family circumstances;
  • communication;
  • participation;
  • motivation; and
  • general presentation.

Possible indicators of emotional or mental-health difficulty may include:

  • significant withdrawal;
  • changes in mood;
  • changes in eating or sleeping patterns;
  • persistent physical complaints;
  • significant changes in behaviour;
  • expressions of hopelessness or worthlessness;
  • significant anxiety;
  • discussion of self-harm or suicide; or
  • other behaviours that cause reasonable concern.

All such concerns should be taken seriously.

Section 16

Reporting and Referral

A simple referral pathway will operate within the school:

01
Notice

A member of staff notices a concern.

02
Report

Shared with the Designated Adult, Wellbeing Lead or Safeguarding Lead.

03
Assess

Staff consider the nature and level of concern.

04
Support

Pastoral, wellbeing, counselling, inclusion, health or specialist support.

05
Review

The situation is reviewed and further action taken if required.

This pathway is informed by the triage approach involving SLT, teachers and the Wellbeing Team already used to determine appropriate support.

16.1 Urgent and Crisis Disclosures

Where a student discloses, or a member of staff otherwise identifies, an immediate risk of self-harm, suicide, or other safeguarding danger, the standard pathway above is set aside in favour of immediate action:

Immediate action

  • the student is not left alone;
  • the Safeguarding/Child Protection Lead (or, if unavailable, the most senior leader on site) is informed immediately, the same day, in person or by phone – not by email alone;
  • parents/carers are contacted in line with the Safeguarding and Child Protection Policy, unless doing so would itself increase risk to the student;
  • a written record of the disclosure, actions taken and decisions made is completed as soon as possible, in the student's own words where practicable; and
  • external emergency services are contacted without delay where there is danger to life.

Staff should not attempt to manage a disclosure of this nature alone or delay reporting it until a scheduled meeting or the end of the school day.

Section 17

Targeted Support

Where universal wellbeing provision is insufficient, PEPS may provide targeted support for individual students or groups. Depending on need, this may include:

  • pastoral support;
  • individual check-ins;
  • small-group support;
  • emotional literacy activities;
  • resilience activities;
  • social skills support;
  • counselling;
  • social work support;
  • learning support;
  • inclusion support;
  • relaxation and mindfulness activities;
  • appropriate therapeutic activities;
  • support following bereavement or significant change;
  • behaviour support; and
  • referral to external services.

This builds on existing provision including targeted group work, emotional support, wellbeing groups, ELSA-type support and therapeutic activities.

Section 18

Specialist and External Support

Where a student's needs exceed the school's internal capacity, PEPS may work with appropriate external professionals and services. These may include, where available and appropriate:

  • educational psychology services;
  • counselling services;
  • health services;
  • child and family services;
  • specialist mental-health services;
  • family support services;
  • behaviour support services; and
  • other relevant professional services.

This builds on the school's existing collaboration with specialist services and external agencies. External referral will be undertaken in accordance with safeguarding, confidentiality, consent and relevant school procedures.

Section 19

Confidentiality and Information Sharing

PEPS will respect the privacy of students, parents and staff when wellbeing concerns are discussed.

However, confidentiality cannot be guaranteed where information indicates that an individual may be at risk of harm or where safeguarding procedures require information to be shared.

Information will be shared appropriately with relevant staff and agencies on a need-to-know basis and in accordance with school procedures and applicable requirements, including the UAE Federal Personal Data Protection Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021).

Wellbeing records are retained, stored and disposed of in line with the school's data protection and records-retention procedures.

Staff should not promise absolute confidentiality to a student who discloses a serious wellbeing or safeguarding concern.

Section 20

Working with Parents and Carers

PEPS recognises parents and carers as important partners in promoting student wellbeing. The school may provide:

  • information about wellbeing;
  • guidance on supporting children at home;
  • information about topics covered through PSHE;
  • workshops and presentations;
  • guidance on examination and academic wellbeing;
  • digital wellbeing guidance;
  • information about healthy routines;
  • signposting to appropriate external support; and
  • opportunities for parents to communicate concerns.

Where a wellbeing concern is identified, parents/carers will normally be involved as appropriate. However, there may be circumstances, particularly those involving safeguarding, where information cannot be shared immediately or in the usual manner.

Section 21

Wellbeing Activities and Events

PEPS will provide opportunities throughout the academic year to promote wellbeing. These may include:

  • wellbeing activities;
  • assemblies;
  • PSHE activities;
  • awareness campaigns;
  • student leadership activities;
  • physical activity initiatives;
  • staff wellbeing activities;
  • parent workshops;
  • resilience activities;
  • digital wellbeing education;
  • examination wellbeing activities; and
  • a dedicated wellbeing week or wellbeing-focused events.

Wellbeing activities will be planned according to the needs and priorities of the school community rather than being limited to a single annual event.

Section 22

Staff Training and Professional Development

PEPS will provide appropriate awareness and professional development opportunities to help staff recognise and respond appropriately to wellbeing concerns. Training may include:

  • understanding student wellbeing;
  • recognising early warning signs;
  • emotional literacy;
  • resilience;
  • supporting students experiencing anxiety or stress;
  • safeguarding and wellbeing;
  • responding appropriately to disclosures;
  • digital wellbeing and online concerns;
  • supporting students through transition;
  • staff wellbeing; and
  • appropriate referral procedures.

Regular staff training on recognising and responding to mental-health issues is required, with additional CPD provided where more specialist knowledge is needed. Training priorities will be determined according to the needs of the school and available resources.

Section 23

Monitoring and Evaluation

PEPS will use a proportionate and manageable approach to monitoring wellbeing. The school will not require a separate complex wellbeing data-management system. The Wellbeing Lead/Wellbeing Team will:

Termly

  • review wellbeing activities undertaken;
  • consider significant wellbeing concerns and emerging themes;
  • review whether appropriate support is being provided; and
  • identify any immediate adjustments required.

Annually

  • review the implementation of this policy;
  • consider feedback from students, staff and parents where available;
  • consider relevant existing school information;
  • evaluate major wellbeing initiatives;
  • identify strengths and areas for development; and
  • recommend priorities for the following academic year.

Existing information such as attendance, behaviour, safeguarding concerns, referrals and feedback may be considered where it is already available through normal school systems. No additional data collection will be introduced unless it is considered necessary and proportionate.

Section 26

Policy Review

This policy will be reviewed annually by the Senior Leadership Team and Wellbeing Team. The review will consider:

  • implementation during the academic year;
  • feedback from relevant stakeholders;
  • emerging wellbeing priorities;
  • changes in school provision;
  • effectiveness of support arrangements;
  • relevant developments affecting school wellbeing practice; and
  • priorities for the following academic year.

The policy may be reviewed earlier where significant changes occur.
