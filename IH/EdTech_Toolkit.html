EdTech Toolkit

A short, actually-useful list — not every tool that exists, just ones worth trying, with a quick way to get started.

✎ Starter list — please confirm each tool against current school IT/data policy before wider use

Using AI tools with students

AI writing and research assistants are genuinely useful when students engage with them critically — checking, questioning, and improving on what a tool produces, rather than accepting it uncritically. This is covered in depth, alongside purposeful technology use more broadly, in the Innovation, Enquiry & Purposeful Technology in the Classroom CPD module.

Open that CPD module →

Writing & Research

2 tools

Padlet

A shared pinboard for brainstorming, gathering sources, or collecting quick responses in real time.

Quick startCreate a board, share the link — students can post directly, no account needed to view.

Google Docs — Explore & Citations

Built into the Google account students already have — search and cite sources without leaving the document.

Quick startTools menu → Explore, or Insert → Citation once a source is added.

Presentation & Media

2 tools

Canva for Education

Drag-and-drop design for posters, presentations, and short videos — free for verified schools.

Quick startSign in with school email to check education-tier access before assigning.

Google Slides

Simple, familiar presentations with real-time collaboration — already available in every student account.

Quick startUse "Share" with comment access for peer feedback before a final version.

Assessment & Feedback

2 tools

Kahoot!

Quick, game-based formative checks — useful for a fast read on understanding mid-lesson.

Quick startStart with a short 5-question check rather than a full quiz — keeps pace tight.

Quizlet

Flashcard sets with practice modes — can be teacher-built or, more powerfully, student-built as a revision task.

Quick startTry having students build the set themselves — the making is often more valuable than the practising.

Collaboration

2 tools

Google Sheets (shared)

A live, shared data collection tool — useful for class surveys, experiment results, or collaborative research logs.

Quick startLock the header row and set data validation to keep entries consistent.

Google Classroom — underused features

Your main platform already supports rubric-based grading and question-and-discussion posts — often underused beyond assignments.

Quick startTry a "Question" post with peer replies enabled for a low-stakes discussion warm-up.
